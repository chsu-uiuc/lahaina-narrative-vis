After the Lahaina Fire, a Different Market Grew Back

West Maui's hotels never quite came back. Something else did.

C.H. · July 2026

On August 8, 2023, a wildfire destroyed most of Lahaina, killing 102 people — the deadliest American wildfire in over a century. Tourism shut down overnight. Yet by that November, West Maui's hotels were fuller than they had been before the fire. The rooms weren't full of tourists. Three years of hotel reports and Airbnb data tell the story of a market that recovered — into something different.

Note: this month's activity window still includes pre-fire reviews.

The market recovered — but not as the same market. Hotels remain below their 2019 norm, while the fastest-growing corner of West Maui lodging now sits within walking distance of the burn zone.

What the listings data cannot say is who owns the new rentals — rebuilt family homes earning income, or investors moving in on the edge of a disaster. Either way, the map of who houses West Maui's visitors has been redrawn.
